## Idea

A function sends an input $x$ to $f(x)$. An equation or an inequality is a condition on that graph.

$$
f(x)=0
$$

The solutions are the intersections with the $x$-axis.

$$
f(x)>0
$$

The solutions are the intervals where the graph lies above the $x$-axis.

The same graph can first be changed by

$$
y=a\,f\bigl(b(x-h)\bigr)+k
$$

and then read again for zeros and for the intervals above the axis. The transform is the picture of a whole curve you can operate on. The quadratic and the polynomial show how the number of zeros, and their multiplicity, change the intersections and the sign intervals.

## Interaction

- **Graph transform**: drag **Vertical scale a**, **Horizontal scale b**, **Horizontal shift h**, and **Vertical shift k**, and compare the ghost of $f(x)$ with the transformed curve. Switch **Basis f(x)** among $x$, $x^2$, $x^3$, and $|x|$
- **Quadratic equation**: drag **Coefficient a**, **Coefficient b**, and **Coefficient c**, and watch the intersections with the $x$-axis, the vertex, and $\Delta=b^2-4ac$ move together
- **Polynomial inequality**: drag **Root r1**, **Root r2**, and **Root r3**. Each of **Multiplicity m1**, **Multiplicity m2**, and **Multiplicity m3** is only 1 or 2. Watch the graph cross or touch the $x$-axis, and read the gold segments on the number line, where the polynomial is positive

Suggested order: Graph transform, then Quadratic equation, then Polynomial inequality.

## What to notice

- $f(x)=0$ and $f(x)>0$ are the intersections and the sign intervals. A quadratic and a polynomial differ in how many zeros they have and in the multiplicity, and you read the graph the same way.
- A transformation first moves and reshapes the whole curve. Asking how it meets the $x$-axis is another reading of that same picture, and the solution set is that reading written out.
- Crossing the $x$-axis or only touching it changes how the sign intervals are cut. The intersections after a transformation are the same language: the graph against the $x$-axis.

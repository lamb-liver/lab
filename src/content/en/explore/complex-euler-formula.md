## Idea

$$
e^{i\theta} = \cos\theta + i\sin\theta
$$

$$
(\cos\theta + i\sin\theta)^n = \cos(n\theta) + i\sin(n\theta)
$$

Multiplying by $e^{i\theta}$ turns an arrow counterclockwise by $\theta$. On this page that same arrow is also a sum, a difference, a product, or a quotient of two complex numbers, and the power in De Moivre's formula. The horizontal projection is $\cos\theta$ and the vertical projection is $\sin\theta$. This is not the separate page that traces only the imaginary part as a sine wave.

## Interaction

- **Complex arithmetic**: switch **Operation** among addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division, and see where $z_1$ and $z_2$ land. Drag the tips of $z_1$ and $z_2$
- **Euler's formula**: drag **Angle θ** and watch $e^{i\theta}$ on the unit circle, together with the cosine and sine projections
- **De Moivre's formula**: compare $(\cos\theta+i\sin\theta)^n$ with $\cos(n\theta)+i\sin(n\theta)$. **Power n** and **Angle θ** move the point at angle $n\theta$

Suggested order: Complex arithmetic, then Euler's formula, then De Moivre's formula.

## What to notice

- Multiplying by a complex number rotates and scales one arrow. Rectangular coordinates are the components of that arrow.
- $e^{i\theta}$ reads the same rotation as a point on the unit circle and as $\cos\theta+i\sin\theta$.
- Addition, multiplication, and rotation share one complex plane. The difference is a shift, or a turn about the origin, not three unrelated operations.

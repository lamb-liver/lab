## Idea

A point stays at a constant ratio of its distance to a focus and its distance to a directrix:

$$
\frac{PF}{Pd} = e
$$

That number is the eccentricity. Below $1$ the curve is an ellipse, at $1$ a parabola, and above $1$ one hyperbola branch tied to that focus and directrix. The circle is only the limit $e \to 0$, and then the directrix ratio is hidden.

## Interaction

- **Mode**: choose **Eccentricity** or **Focus locus**
- **e**: move the ratio from a near-circle ellipse, through a parabola at $e = 1$, to one hyperbola branch
- **Show focus / directrix**: draw the focus, the directrix, and the point $P$. In the circle limit the directrix is left off, and the status hides the distance ratio
- **Curve**: in **Focus locus**, choose **Ellipse**, **Parabola**, or **Hyperbola**
- **Animate point P**: let $P$ travel on its own, or drag $P$ on the figure

Suggested order: start with a small **e**, near a circle, then $e = 1$, then $e > 1$ for the single branch. Switch **Mode** to **Focus locus** and compare **Ellipse**, **Parabola**, and **Hyperbola**. The hyperbola in that mode draws both branches.

## What to notice

- The same ratio $e$ picks the family. Less than $1$, equal to $1$, and greater than $1$ are one rule crossing a threshold, not three definitions.
- As $e$ grows, the closed curve opens and then settles on the one branch that matches the focus and the directrix. Raising $e$ does not split the drawing into two branches.
- **Focus locus** reads the same curves another way. An ellipse keeps a constant sum of distances to two foci, a parabola keeps equal distances to the focus and the directrix, and a hyperbola keeps a constant absolute difference, with both branches drawn.

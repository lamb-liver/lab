## Parametric equations

Start from a basic function $f$. The general transformation is

$$
y=a\,f\bigl(b(x-h)\bigr)+k
$$

- $h$ and $k$ translate the graph horizontally and vertically.
- $a$ scales it vertically. When $a<0$, the graph flips upside down.
- $b$ scales it horizontally. $|b|>1$ compresses the graph along $x$, and $0<|b|<1$ stretches it. $b<0$ reflects the input left to right. The graphs of $x$ and $x^3$ flip; $x^2$ and $|x|$ look the same.

The figure draws the faint base curve and the transformed curve together, so the same rule can be compared as the shape changes.

## Interaction

- **Basis f(x)**: switch among $x$, $x^2$, $x^3$, and $|x|$, and watch the same rule on different shapes
- **Horizontal shift h** and **Vertical shift k**: drag the sliders, or drag **P** on the figure, and the graph moves left and right or up and down
- **Vertical scale a** and **Horizontal scale b**: drag them and watch the opening and the horizontal compression or stretch. $a<0$ flips the graph upside down. $b<0$ reflects the input, which shows on $x$ and $x^3$

## What to notice

- $h$ and $k$ move the graph and leave its shape alone. $a$ and $b$ change the opening and the width, and the sign of $a$ flips the graph upside down.
- The same $(a,b,h,k)$ follows one rule on every basis. How bent $f$ is changes what that rule looks like.
- The vertex form $a(x-h)^2+k$ is this transformation applied to $y=x^2$.

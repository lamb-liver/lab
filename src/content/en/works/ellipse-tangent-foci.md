## Parametric equations

The major axis has length 2. The point of tangency is $P=(\cos t,\, b\sin t)$, with $b=\sqrt{1-e^{2}}$. The eccentricity $e$ runs from $0.25$ to $0.85$, and the foci are $(\pm e,\, 0)$. The angle $t$ is measured from the positive major axis and runs from $20^\circ$ to $160^\circ$, so $P$ stays on the upper half.

The tangent at $P$ makes equal angles with the focal radii $PF_1$ and $PF_2$. The two radii add up to 2.

$$
x^{2}+\frac{y^{2}}{b^{2}}=1
$$

## Interaction

- **Position t**: move the point of tangency along the ellipse; both focal radii and the tangent turn with it
- **Eccentricity e**: change how flat the ellipse is; the foci move apart or together

## What to notice

- The tangent makes the same angle with each focal radius.
- The foci lie on the major axis, and the distances from the point to the two foci add up to the whole major axis.
- A flatter ellipse puts the foci nearer the ends of the major axis, and the angles stay equal.

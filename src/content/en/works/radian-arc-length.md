## Figure

On a circle of radius $r$, the arc subtended by a central angle $\theta$ in radians has length

$$
s=r\theta
$$

On the unit circle ($r=1$), the radian measure and the arc length are the same number. Degrees convert to radians by

$$
\theta_{\text{rad}}=\theta_{\text{deg}}\cdot\frac{\pi}{180^\circ}
$$

## Interaction

- **Angle θ**: drag the point on the circle or the slider to change $\theta$; the arc length $s$, the radian value, and the degree value update together
- A colored arc marks the arc from $(r,0)$ to $P(\theta)$. Its length is $s=r\theta$. On the unit circle that length equals the radian measure.
- **r = 1** and **r = 2**: switch the radius and watch the arc length scale with the radius at the same angle
- **Special angles: on**: mark $\pi/6$, $\pi/4$, $\pi/3$, $\pi/2$, and $\pi$ on the circle

## What to notice

- The radian measure ties the size of an angle to arc length. On the unit circle, reading the radian measure is reading the arc length.
- Double the radius and the arc length doubles. The angle itself, in radians, does not change with the size of the circle.
- A negative angle traces the arc the other way, and the absolute value of the angle is still $|\theta|$.

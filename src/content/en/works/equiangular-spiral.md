## Parametric equations

An equiangular spiral (a logarithmic spiral) can be written:

$$
r = ae^{b\theta}
$$

In Cartesian coordinates:

$$
x = ae^{b\theta}\cos\theta,\quad
y = ae^{b\theta}\sin\theta
$$

Here $a>0$. This figure uses $b>0$, and the angle between the tangent and a ray from the origin is the constant $\arctan(1/b)$. If $b=0$, the curve degenerates to a circle.

## Interaction

- **Growth coefficient b**: control how fast the spiral opens outward
- **Maximum angle θ**: limit how much of the spiral is shown; the bright arc runs out along the angle, and a faint copy keeps the full range
- **Rotation speed**: rotate the whole figure slowly about the origin

## What to notice

- The radius $r=ae^{b\theta}$ keeps the angle between the curve and any ray from the origin constant.
- For $b>0$, increasing $\theta$ spreads successive turns by an exponential factor.
- Rotating the view does not change that angle. It only changes the direction you are looking from.

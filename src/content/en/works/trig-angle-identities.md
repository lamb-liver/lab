## Parametric equations

The angles $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are two directions on the unit circle. A sum-to-product identity rewrites a sum or a difference as a product in the midpoint angle $m=\dfrac{\alpha+\beta}{2}$ and the offset $d=\dfrac{\alpha-\beta}{2}$. For example,

$$
\sin\alpha+\sin\beta
=
2\sin\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}\cos\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2}
$$

$$
\cos\alpha-\cos\beta
=
-2\sin\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}\sin\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2}
$$

The angle-addition formula $\sin(\alpha+\beta)$ and the double-angle formula $\sin 2\alpha$ are other algebraic readings of the same angle composition. This figure stays with the four sum-to-product identities, and with the product-to-sum reading in the other direction.

## Interaction

- **sin+**: $\sin\alpha+\sin\beta=2\sin m\cos d$. The sum shows the mean direction
- **sin−**: $\sin\alpha-\sin\beta=2\cos m\sin d$. The difference shows the half-difference vector
- **cos+**: $\cos\alpha+\cos\beta=2\cos m\cos d$. The sum shows the mean direction
- **cos−**: $\cos\alpha-\cos\beta=-2\sin m\sin d$. The difference shows the half-difference vector
- **Angle α**: drag this endpoint on the circle, or use the slider
- **Angle β**: drag the other endpoint, or use the slider
- **Composition guide: on**: mark $m=\dfrac{\alpha+\beta}{2}$ and $d=\dfrac{\alpha-\beta}{2}$
- **Reading: sum-to-product**: read the same figure forward as sum-to-product, or backward as product-to-sum
- **Angle display: degrees**: show the angles in degrees or in radians

## What to notice

- Sum-to-product comes from the midpoint angle and the offset of the two directions. It is not a list of unrelated formulas.
- When $\alpha\approx\beta$, the offset $d$ goes to $0$, and the figure marks $\alpha\approx\beta$.
- Product-to-sum and sum-to-product are the forward and backward readings of the same geometric relation.

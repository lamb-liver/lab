## Figure

A quadratic ($a\neq 0$) can be written in general form

$$
f(x)=ax^2+bx+c
$$

or in vertex form

$$
f(x)=a(x-h)^2+k
$$

where the vertex is $V=(h,k)$. The discriminant

$$
\Delta=b^2-4ac
$$

decides whether the graph meets the $x$-axis in two points, touches it at one point, or does not meet it in the real numbers.

## Interaction

- **Coefficient a**, **Coefficient b**, and **Coefficient c**: drag $a$, $b$, and $c$ and watch the opening, the position, and the $x$-intercepts
- **V**: the vertex and the axis $x=h$ stay marked. Drag **V** on the figure and the coefficients $b$ and $c$ follow
- **Completing guide: on** / **Completing guide: off**: show the base parabola $y=ax^2$ and, once the vertex has moved, the arrow from the origin to the vertex
- **Two real roots** / **Repeated root** / **No real roots**: switch the three discriminant cases and compare the sign of $\Delta$ with the $x$-axis

## What to notice

- $a$ sets the direction of the opening and how wide it is. A larger $|a|$ makes the parabola narrower.
- The vertex form gives the shift directly. The general form is the one that computes $\Delta$ and the intercepts from the coefficients.
- $\Delta>0$ gives two intercepts, $\Delta=0$ means the graph touches the $x$-axis, and $\Delta<0$ means the graph misses the $x$-axis, so the equation has no real solution.

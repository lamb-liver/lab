## Figure

Adding complex numbers translates and combines vectors in the plane. Multiplying them adds the arguments and multiplies the moduli. The moving arrows show the algebra and the geometry at the same time.

Let $z_1=r_1 e^{i\theta_1}$ and $z_2=r_2 e^{i\theta_2}$:

$$
z_1+z_2 \Rightarrow (x_1+x_2,\, y_1+y_2),\quad
z_1 z_2 \Rightarrow r_1 r_2\, e^{i(\theta_1+\theta_2)}
$$

## Interaction

- **z₁ modulus r₁** and **z₁ argument θ₁**: set the modulus and argument of $z_1$
- **z₂ modulus r₂** and **z₂ argument θ₂**: set the modulus and argument of $z_2$
- The sum $z_1+z_2$ lies on the diagonal of the parallelogram, and the product $z_1 z_2$ multiplies the moduli and adds the arguments

## What to notice

- Adding complex numbers is adding vectors in the plane.
- In polar form, multiplying complex numbers is a scaling and a rotation composed together.
- The unit circle is a reference scale of modulus 1.

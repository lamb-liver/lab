## Figure

The angle $\theta$ corresponds to the point on the unit circle

$$
P(\theta) = (\cos\theta,\ \sin\theta)
$$

The tangent is $\tan\theta=\sin\theta/\cos\theta$ when $\cos\theta\neq 0$. Because $P$ lies on the circle of radius $1$, $\sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta=1$ always holds.

- **θ**: the size of the angle; the button reads **Angle display: degrees** or **Angle display: radians**
- **P**: the corresponding point on the unit circle

## Interaction

- **Angle θ**: drag the point on the circle or the slider to change $\theta$; $\cos\theta$, $\sin\theta$, and $\tan\theta$ update together
- **Quadrant signs: on**: mark each of the four quadrants with the sign of each function, the usual quadrant pattern
- **Special angles: on**: show the exact values at $30^\circ$, $45^\circ$, $60^\circ$, and the other marked angles; the marks stay fixed on the circle
- Perpendiculars from $P$ to the two axes show the lengths $\cos\theta$ and $\sin\theta$

## What to notice

- $\sin\theta$ is the y-coordinate and $\cos\theta$ is the x-coordinate, and the sum of their squares is always $1$.
- $\tan\theta$ can be read as the length of a segment on the tangent to the unit circle. As $\theta\to 90^\circ{}^-$, the tangent tends to $+\infty$, and at $\theta=90^\circ$ it is undefined.
- Negative angles and angles past $360^\circ$ repeat a point on the circle, and that is where the periodicity comes from.

## Figure

Euler's formula $e^{i\theta}=\cos\theta+i\sin\theta$ says that multiplying by $e^{i\theta}$ is a counterclockwise rotation by $\theta$. In the figure, the tip of the rotating vector stays in step with the sine wave of its imaginary part on the right, tying the exponential to the trigonometric functions.

$$
e^{i\theta} = \cos\theta + i\sin\theta
$$

$$
z(\theta) = e^{i\theta},\quad \theta\in[0,2\pi)
$$

In general, $re^{i\theta}$ is the complex number of modulus $r$ and argument $\theta$.

## Interaction

- **Amplitude A**: change the length of the rotating vector and the amplitude of the sine wave on the right
- **Angular frequency ω**: control how fast the vector turns and how tightly the wave is packed
- **Initial phase δ**: shift where the rotation starts and the phase of the wave

## What to notice

- The imaginary part of the rotating vector on the complex plane is the height of the sine wave in time.
- $e^{i(\omega t+\delta)}$ writes the circular motion and the trigonometric functions in one expression.
- The angular frequency sets both the period of the rotation and the spatial period of the wave.

## Parametric equations

A Lissajous curve has the form

$$
x = A \sin(at + \delta),\quad
y = B \sin(bt)
$$

- **A** and **B** are the amplitudes on x and y. This figure keeps A = B, so the orbit stays inside a square.
- **a** and **b** are the frequencies. The ratio a:b is the ratio of those two controls, and it sets the crossings and the complexity.
- **δ** is the phase difference. It sets the symmetry and the sense of rotation or opening.

## Interaction

- **Frequency a**: change the integer frequency on x and watch the crossings of the ratio a:b
- **Frequency b**: change the integer frequency on y. The ratio a:b is these two controls
- **Phase δ**: drag the phase difference. The orbit deforms continuously between a locked shape and a twisted one

## What to notice

- Only the reduced ratio matters: 2:4 draws the same curve as 1:2. The larger the reduced pair a:b, the more lobes and crossings, and the more of the square the curve covers. A simple ratio stays more symmetric.
- $\delta$ changes how the figure sits against the axes. It does not change the ratio a:b.
- With A = B the orbit stays inside a square, which makes different ratios a:b easy to compare.

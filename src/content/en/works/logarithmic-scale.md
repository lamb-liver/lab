## Parametric equations

A logarithmic scale puts $\log_{10} y$ (or $\ln y$) on the vertical axis, so each factor of ten in $y$ is the same vertical step. Earthquake magnitude, decibels, and pH compress a wide range this way. The figure always shows the same curves twice: a linear axis on the left, and a log axis on the right.

$$
y=10^{x}\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad \log_{10} y = x
$$

An exponential $y=Ce^{kt}$ is a straight line on the $\ln y$ axis:

$$
\ln y = \ln C + kt
$$

## Interaction

- **Exponential slope a**: change $a$, and compare the bend on the linear axis with the fixed slope on the log axis
- **Compare**: overlay **Exponential**, **Power**, and **Linear** on both axes
- **Power p** and **Linear factor m**: set the power and the straight line once those curves are on

The two axes stay side by side. **Compare** does not create them.

## What to notice

- An equal step on the log axis is a factor of ten. On the linear axis the same numbers pile up near the bottom.
- The exponential is the curve that becomes straight. The power and the linear curve stay bent on the log axis.
- The two panels use the same values. What changes is whether an equal step is a difference or a ratio.

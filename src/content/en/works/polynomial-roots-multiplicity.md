## Figure

A polynomial in factored form:

$$
f(x)=a(x-r_1)^{m_1}(x-r_2)^{m_2}(x-r_3)^{m_3}
$$

Each $r_i$ is a zero, and each multiplicity $m_i$ is $1$ or $2$. At $x=r_i$, $f(r_i)=0$, so the graph meets the x-axis there.

The equation $f(x)=0$ asks where the zeros are. The inequality $f(x)>0$ asks which intervals sit above the x-axis.

## Interaction

- **Three crossings** / **One double root** / **Two touches**: jump among three simple zeros, one double root in the middle, and two touches
- **Leading coefficient a**: change the leading coefficient. The sign of $a$ flips which intervals are positive
- **Zero r₁**, **Zero r₂**, and **Zero r₃**: drag a zero, or drag its mark on the figure
- **Multiplicity m₁**, **Multiplicity m₂**, and **Multiplicity m₃**: each switch is only $1$ or $2$. Multiplicity $1$ crosses the x-axis. Multiplicity $2$ touches and turns back
- **Multiplicity guide**: mark each zero as crosses or touches. The sign line under the graph stays on: gold where $f(x)>0$, gray where $f(x)<0$

## What to notice

- The zeros cut the number line into intervals. The sign on an interval comes from the leading coefficient $a$ together with the multiplicities.
- Multiplicity $1$ crosses the x-axis. Multiplicity $2$ only touches, so the sign is the same on both sides.
- A quadratic is the special case of at most two zeros, each of multiplicity $1$ or $2$. The discriminant counts the real roots. The multiplicity says whether the graph crosses or touches.

## Figure

A general sinusoid

$$
y=A\sin\!\left(\frac{2\pi}{T}(x-\phi)\right)+k
$$

- **A** is the vertical scale. The amplitude is $|A|$, and $A<0$ flips the graph across the center line.
- **T** is the period, the horizontal distance from one peak to the next.
- **φ** is the phase shift. It moves the graph along the x-axis.
- **k** moves the center line up or down.

The base sine wave $y=\sin x$ is the case $A=1$, $T=2\pi$, $\phi=0$, $k=0$.

## Interaction

- **Vertical scale A**: drag the slider and watch the amplitude $|A|$, and the flip when $A<0$, against the base sine comparison curve
- **Period T**: change the period and watch the wave compress or stretch, so the window holds more or fewer peaks
- **Phase shift φ**: move where the wave starts, and watch it slip left or right of the base sine comparison curve
- **Center line k**: shift the whole curve up or down. The guide marks $y=k$
- **Compare: on**: show or hide the base sine wave $y=\sin x$
- **Guides: on**: show or hide the center line, the band from $k-|A|$ to $k+|A|$, the phase line, and the period bracket
- The sidebar shows $A$, $T$, $\phi$, and $k$. The figure marks the current peak. When $A=0$ the curve collapses to the center line and there is no peak.

## What to notice

- $|A|$ sets the amplitude, and the sign of $A$ decides the flip. $T$ only changes the horizontal scale. The two are independent.
- $\phi$ is a phase shift along the x-axis. It does not change the amplitude or the period.
- The angular frequency $\omega=2\pi/T$ ties the period to that horizontal scale. A larger $T$ spreads the graph out.
